# Review checks and corrections

Use the graph to find candidate pairs or groups, then assess them against the source and context:

- **Time and status:** as-of date, completed versus planned, revision or phase, before/after sequence, legal status and jurisdiction.
- **Quantities:** recompute totals, percentages, growth rates and denominators; compare like units, period length, rounding and explicitly stated assumptions. A changed projection may be valid if the assumptions or periods differ.
- **Scope:** full population versus a selected subgroup, worldwide versus local, market size versus company revenue, a sample versus its population, product target versus measured result, component versus whole system.
- **Technical claims:** distinguish known facts from assumptions; compare test environment, baselines, distribution or state, scale and uncertainty. Avoid turning an absence of evidence into a universal impossibility.
- **Ownership and rights:** distinguish ownership, licenses, applications, grants and territorial validity; check authoritative records before making a legal-status claim.
- **Cross-references:** reconcile summaries, detailed sections, tables, figures and captions; check that update-related edits are carried consistently across the document.

For each finding, identify source location(s), quote the relevant wording accurately, explain the mismatched condition or calculation, and provide replacement text that can be copied. Make unknown facts explicit with clear placeholders or neutral wording; never fabricate a missing result or contract. For a suspected issue, explain what needs checking rather than declaring it an error. Link relevant primary sources close to the finding when external validation is used; distinguish the source's statement from your inference.
